    Why we use a control group

    Naive before/after: Everyone's output rose anyway: AI effect (adopters minus non-adopters):

    Output rose for people who never used AI too (busy season, new product). A plain before/after would credit all of it to AI. Each person is compared with their own first two months, as a ratio, so an intern and a senior are comparable. Limit: people choose whether to adopt, so adopters may differ from non-adopters in ways we cannot see.

    ROI by department, with 95% range

    "Uncertain" means the range includes zero: the data cannot tell this department from break-even. "Break-even credit" is the share of the estimated gain that must be real for the department to pay for itself.

    ROI by level

    Value is time saved times hourly rate, so lower-paid levels show lower dollar ROI on the same seat. This is not a verdict on their work, and it ignores learning and ramp-up. Peer comparisons for overspend are made within level, never across.
