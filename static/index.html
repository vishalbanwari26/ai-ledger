    What each policy assumes

      Recommended budget per department

      "Expected under plan" is the average cost per head after reassigning unused seats. "Ceiling" is the most one active person may use, so it is higher than an average on purpose. Caps are peer norms: what colleagues of the same role and level who get a positive return spend today. They describe use, not need. Interns and juniors are never set below the department cap, because they iterate more while learning. Treat every cap as a starting point to adjust with the team.

      Cost by model: route routine work to a cheaper one

      Prices and shares: frontier priceⓘ, cheaper modelⓘ, quality penaltyⓘ, routine shareⓘ.

      Define what AI output is worth to you

      AI value is: extra output (measured from your data) x what one unit of output is worth x the share you count as real (the realization sliderⓘ). The tool measures the first part. You decide the second, per department. By default a unit is worth the hours it takes times an hourly rateⓘ.

      Ways to value a unit: time saved at a loaded hourly rate (the default); the handling cost avoided per ticket; the expected margin of a qualified sales opportunity; what a contract review costs externally. Use your own figures: the tool does not know them.

      Blank fields use the data's own figures (shown in gray). "All departments" fills in for any department you leave alone. An extra benefit per user is for value that is not extra output, such as retention or quality; it is counted in full and is not reduced by the realization share.

      What counts as one unit of output?

      Name the unit your team really produces, and if you track several things, say how much each counts. For example, one pull request = 1, one code review = 0.3, one commit = 0.1. The tool then measures the AI effect on that mix.

      Your CSV supplies the metrics: the required outcome_units column, plus any extra outcome_<name> columns. A weighted mix is a new unit, so also set what one is worth in the table above (dollars per unit, or hours per unit). Blank weights mean 1 for the main metric and 0 for the others.

      The tool's judgement calls, as settings

      These are rules of thumb built into the tool: when a seat counts as unused, what counts as high spend, how many months to measure, what a fixed budget caps. Change them to match how your organization works. Each shows its default and where it comes from (ⓘ). Risky settings are allowed but come with a warning that says what the stress tests found.

      The sliders in "Data and assumptions" cover the value share, elasticity, the cheaper model's price and the pay-per-use price. Value and output are on the Your value tab.

      Share of tokens that are routine work, by department

      Used by the model-routing estimate: how much of each department's use is summaries, drafts and lookups that a smaller model could do. Measure it on your own traffic before relying on it.

      Why we use a control group

      Naive before/after: Everyone's output rose anyway: AI effect (adopters minus non-adopters):

      Baseline and measured monthsⓘ; pre-rollout checkⓘ.

      Output rose for people who never used AI too (busy season, new product). A plain before/after would credit all of it to AI. Each person is compared with their own first two months, as a ratio, so an intern and a senior are comparable. Limit: people choose whether to adopt. We test whether adopters were already pulling ahead before rollout and remove that gap, but differences that only start at rollout cannot be seen.

      ROI by department, with 95% range

      "Uncertain" means the range includes zero: the data cannot tell this department from break-even. "Break-even credit" is the share of the estimated gain that must be real for the department to pay for itself.

      ROI by level

      Value is time saved times hourly rate, so lower-paid levels show lower dollar ROI on the same seat. This is not a verdict on their work, and it ignores learning and ramp-up. Peer comparisons for high spend are made within level, never across.
